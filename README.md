# MT — Master Trade

`MT.mq5` is a MetaTrader 5 Expert Advisor with a market panel, independent timeframe readings, informational trade plans and an observed-market diary. It does not place or manage orders. Copy `MT.mq5` **and all five `MT_*.mqh` files** into the same `MQL5/Experts` folder, compile in MetaEditor, and attach it to a supported chart. Phase 3 source is implemented but native compilation, broker integration and out-of-sample target performance have not been verified.

## Panel

The default appearance remains Consolas 10 pt on `C'48,48,48'`, with the title in white at 11 pt. The EA measures the displayed text using MT5 font metrics and sizes the box to its current content, including the detail/notice line; a conservative DPI-based estimate is used if text measurement fails. Supported charts reserve three header rows followed by the three timeframe lanes. Row 1 contains the header; row 2 is blank when open and shows market details when closed or abnormal; row 3 remains reserved. Width and height are measured to cover all displayed content. Long lines are drawn as multiple short labels to avoid text truncation.

- M5, M15 and M30: the header shows the broker's symbol, its best-effort asset class and market state. While open it shows server time in the header and the three timeframe lanes below the three-row header area; row 2 is blank, with no clock-estimation guidance. When closed it shows `Open at` and the next regular session, plus an Automate-style `MARKET CLOSED - next open ... server (in ...)` detail.
- Other timeframes: the header identifies the symbol and timeframe and the two-line panel shows SimpleEA's `Incompatible chart. Switch to M5/M15/M30.` notice.
- The weekly broker trade-session table is refreshed every five minutes. Sessions may cross midnight. If it is unavailable, a fresh quote can indicate an open market; without a fresh quote the state is unknown and `Open at --` is shown rather than an invented opening time. Disconnection, broker-disabled symbols, and stale quotes are distinguished from a scheduled close.

The next opening is a **regular weekly schedule**, not a guarantee: holidays, broker halts, and changed schedules can invalidate it. `00:00–00:00` is treated as a 24-hour session. Classification uses broker metadata and name heuristics and can be wrong; it does not affect trading.

## Phase 2 readings

Each lane is calculated independently for the chart symbol on the **closed bars** of M5, M15 or M30:

- **GT**: Automate's 30-bar regression slope normalized by the median absolute change of 20 bars; UP/DOWN outside ±0.20, otherwise FLAT.
- **CT**: SimpleEA's adaptive Precision Trend filter sign. Its adaptive short cycle is 14–36 (24 when there are too few crossings); the long period is approximately 6.25 times the short, clamped to 90–225. CT is FLAT at exactly zero.
- **ST**: matching directions give UP or DOWN, two FLATs give FLAT, opposed directions give CONFLICT, and one FLAT with a direction gives MIXED. An unavailable component gives WAIT.
- **RG**: SimpleEA's Hurst estimator on that lane's own data, never a borrowed timeframe. It uses 1,536/512/256 bars for M5/M15/M30, plus the preceding close. Above 0.55 is STEADY, below 0.45 CHOPPY, otherwise MIXED. Invalid data or insufficient history gives WAIT, not a substituted 0.5.
- **SC**: SimpleEA's three votes: CT direction, its ATR-deadbanded rate of change, and 20-bar net movement with confirmed swing checks and an ATR deadband. It ranges from -3/3 to +3/3. GT, ST, RG and PT do not change the score.

**PT** estimates the remaining life of the selected label: M5 CT, M15 ST and M30 GT. **%L** beside SC independently estimates the share of the exact signed score's estimated life remaining. Each uses the median remaining length of at least 20 fully observed past runs of the same label that reached the current age, including zero remaining. A run beginning before the replay window is not counted. Historical score and adaptive periods are reconstructed causally, without training or waiting for new trades. Estimates can be inaccurate or biased by changes in market conditions or the available historical window.

The countdown basis is held when a state starts or the EA attaches mid-state. A positive life fraction that rounds below one percent is shown as `<1%L`, never as an expired zero. Each subsequent closed bar reduces the remaining count. PT shows `~0b` at its estimate and `0b+` afterward; score life shows `~0%L` and then `past est.`. Each first label change resets the respective countdown, including `+3` to `+2`. Terminal global variables preserve a valid basis across restarts where possible; MT verifies the observed label and age across the intervening bars before restoring it. `--`/`--%L` means that duration is unavailable, not zero. **Neither PT nor %L is a trade-success probability or a guarantee of a change.**

MT requests closed history directly from the terminal, retries synchronization and progressively increases the request when estimates lack samples, up to the **terminal's configured maximum bars per timeframe** (excluding the forming bar). The broker's history, synchronization, terminal settings, invalid data and available memory can limit what is available. If a deeper `CopyRates` request fails, the EA retries the deeper request and can retain independently supported readings from a smaller available range; it never substitutes a regime value. With short history, already supported GT or RG may display while CT/SC still show WAIT. The filter uses a 700-bar warm-up and the finite historical replay can differ slightly from SimpleEA's live rolling pull because recursive filter initialization is not identical. Large history requests and calculations can take time, memory and CPU and may briefly block MT5; data are refreshed on new bars and incomplete history is retried. Phase 2 itself does not place orders; Phase 3 adds the inputs and informational planning described below.

## Server clock

MT5 has one trade-server time for the account, not a separate clock per symbol. In `OnTick`, `TimeCurrent()` is the server-stamped time of the chart symbol's handled quote. Outside `OnTick`, it is the most recent quote time for **any** Market Watch symbol. The panel anchors to new broker timestamps and advances between them using a monotonic counter. This avoids the previous reliance on `TimeTradeServer()`, which MetaTrader calculates from the PC's clock settings. After startup and before a new quote is observed, `TimeTradeServer()` is the only running estimate available. In Strategy Tester, simulated `TimeCurrent()` is used instead of real elapsed time.

A timestamp in a quote has second precision and may be delayed in transit; extrapolation between quotes and over long closures can drift, and a server clock or timezone change can occur without a new quote. **Perfect synchronization cannot be guaranteed by MT5's APIs.** The clock is estimated between quotes. Compare it with Market Watch's server time on a live connection, and check the PC clock if the panel starts before any fresh quote.

## Phase 3 — informational plans and diary

The panel preserves the three header rows and Phase 2 lanes, then shows three plan lines, the symbol's bid/ask and three diary summaries. `Show diary details` adds one detail line per timeframe. Plan lines contain BUY/SELL/NONE, market or limit entry, lot, SL and TP prices and estimated SL/TP amounts in account currency. Plan amounts are gross `OrderCalcProfit` estimates; a price outcome is not an executed trade. The box still resizes for all rows and label segments.

Inputs: `Trend to follow M5/M15/M30` are four-choice inputs (Auto/CT/GT/ST), default Auto; `Allow against trend M5/M15/M30` default true. `Bypass risk`, `Minimum lot use`, and `Show diary details` default false. Normal sizing targets 0.5% of equity per plan, using the entry/SL loss estimated by `OrderCalcProfit`; broker min/max/step apply and below-minimum lots become the broker minimum. Minimum lot use forces that minimum. Bypass uses SimpleEA's balance ladder (0.01 lot per 1,000 account-currency balance units, 1.5 times at absolute score 3). It is not normalized across currencies. Inputs affect the EA but do not enable trading.

A score of ±3 uses current Ask/Bid for market entry; ±2 uses a confirmed swing limit or a fallback derived from the median recent directional bar move. SL uses a confirmed opposite swing with spread/tick protection, or a recent-move fallback. For TP, MT reconstructs completed same-signed-score historical bar analogues, measures favorable movement before the stop over 4/6/8 bars for M5/M15/M30, and uses the median favorable movement relative to stop risk when at least 20 analogues exist. On Bid-based charts, a limit touch is assessed using bar-level spread proxies; stop wins ambiguous bars, and the entry bar contributes no favorable extreme. On Last-based charts, or when the chart price type is unavailable, Bid-specific replay is disabled; recent-move fallback and chart-bar structure are used as a clearly labelled Last/unknown price proxy. With insufficient analogues or a zero median it also uses a recent-move fallback (one tick if no positive directional bar movement is available). TP is capped by an opposing pivot where compatible with broker-distance constraints. These are initial design choices, not optimized or validated claims. Historical replay is not the diary, and does not use future bars at each historical decision. Entry and plan geometry can be unavailable if required quotes, history or contract data are unavailable; BUY/SELL remains visible.

The diary records observed broker quote touches, not fills or realized profit. It counts one idea when a lane enters a new directional signal, rather than on every tick; the same direction is not re-counted until it changes or becomes NONE. If a plan or tick snapshot is unavailable, it records `AWAIT DATA` and can start that same idea later from a current real quote while its direction remains current. Original signal context and time and later plan context and time are preserved separately; no earlier touch is inferred. Once a plan starts, entry, SL and TP are frozen. Awaiting/pending/open/expired/unknown are not wins or losses. Awaiting and untouched pending observations expire if their lane changes direction or becomes NONE; open observations continue to their original TP/SL. Signals count on their broker-server signal day, wins/losses on their resolution day, and all-time begins with the local diary. The live file is in MT5 Common Files keyed by account, server and symbol; tester files are isolated. It uses a single-writer lock and versioned snapshot. Existing version-3 and version-4 diary records migrate to version 5; version-3 bar-price provenance remains unknown. A second instance cannot record to the same diary. `CopyTicksRange` requests intermediate bid/ask ticks; failure, disconnection, or a mismatch between the tick-history endpoint and quote snapshot makes affected active outcomes unknown. Open/pending observations become unknown on restart because continuous coverage is not certified. MT5 may supply incomplete tick history even without an identifiable error, so the diary is not a broker execution ledger. Hiding details does not stop recording. Changing an MT5 input reinitializes the EA, so already active observations become unknown rather than claiming continuous coverage.

Run `python3 tests/test_phase3_kernels.py`, `python3 tests/test_plan_kernels.py`, `python3 tests/test_plan_runtime.py`, `python3 tests/test_diary_kernels.py` and `python3 tests/test_diary_runtime.py` in addition to the earlier suites. These test actual source through C++ stubs, not MetaEditor or a broker. Test on a demo terminal: compile all files; inspect plan prices, tick size, stops and account-currency amounts; check three timeframe inputs and countertrend switches; compare recorded touch order with tick history; test restart, connection loss, two charts, file corruption and broker-day rollover.
